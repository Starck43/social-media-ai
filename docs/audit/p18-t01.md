## What was delivered

Pairing with an independently compared device fingerprint, an outbound mTLS channel carrying signed closed-schema envelopes, a six-entry operation allowlist with proposed ceilings, revocation epochs, a digest-bound replay ledger, traversal/TOCTOU defences and a threat model with residual limitations. No inbound port is required at any step.

## Independent review of the eight questions

Reviewed by the accepting auditor against the published brief on 2026-11-03. Every verdict below is a reading of `docs/LOCAL_CONNECTOR_PROTOCOL.md`; **none is evidence from a running system**, and none exercises a real provider, certificate authority or device key store.

| # | Question | Verdict | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Can a valid certificate release a file the actor may no longer read? | **Addressed, not proven.** Certificate validity is explicitly not authorization: rights are rechecked at issuance *and* before release, and "TLS alone does not establish the actor's current authorization" is stated. No mechanism is specified, by design. | §26–35, §101–102, §106–109 |
| 2 | Is `local_only` enforced before reading, or after sending? | **Addressed at the contract level.** Non-egress covers paths, metadata, queries, snippets and derived bytes; "execute locally or report unavailable"; no permissive fallback. Order is implied by "execute locally", not spelled out as a sequencing requirement. | §37–47 |
| 3 | Revocation between authorization and release | **Addressed.** In-flight work is cancelled, epoch and rights are rechecked before release, and serialization is required where needed. Residual risk stated honestly. | §167–175 |
| 4 | Offline connector that never receives the notice | **Addressed, with a named limitation.** Server-side denial is declared *the* security boundary, and wiping an unreachable device is explicitly not promised. Correct framing. | §177–180 |
| 5 | Does the server ever send a path that becomes authority? | **Addressed.** "No arbitrary path supplied by the server is authority"; ids are opaque and resolve only through the approved root on the device. | §35, §149–155 |
| 6 | Replay ledger scoping and digest collisions | **Addressed.** Ledger is device-scoped and durable across restarts; same id with a different digest is rejected; ambiguous outcomes stay `unknown` rather than success. TTL and capacity are deferred to review, fail-closed. | §110–118 |
| 7 | Can a hijacked owner session confirm a foreign key fingerprint? | **Addressed as an acknowledged limit.** Fingerprint comparison is bound to the exact pairing id, tenant, owner and public key, and the text states plainly that a compromised owner session or UI remains a risk. No stronger claim is made. | §62–67 |
| 8 | TOCTOU between path validation and read | **Addressed, implementation-dependent.** Handle-relative/no-follow operations, revalidation at use and "where safe enforcement cannot be established, deny". No guarantee is claimed for platforms without those primitives. | §149–155 |

### The gap that matters most

Verdicts 1, 3 and 8 are the ones a real deployment usually breaks. The document states the right obligation in each case; the obligation is met by rechecking, and rechecking is exactly what an implementation can omit while still passing a naive happy-path test. P18 implementation tests must target these three specifically, not just the allowlist and size ceilings.

### What this review did not do

- No code, no test, no provider call: nothing here can confirm a property holds.
- No second opinion: one reader, one reading.
- No judgement on the deferred crypto choices (canonical encoding, signature suite, trust anchors, ledger bounds).
